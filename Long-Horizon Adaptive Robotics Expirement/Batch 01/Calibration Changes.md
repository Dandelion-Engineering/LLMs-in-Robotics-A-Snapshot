## Public arm interfaces, initial validity and finite parking

The optional public arm boundary now admits bounded joint position, velocity and desired effort commands, timestamped joint trajectories, and Cartesian targets through the same numerical IK. These permissions are common to all four entrants; the selected policies may continue using task motions. Direct commands have at most two seconds of authority and at most 100 Hz admission. Explicit same-incarnation replacement or request-correlated cancellation revokes the old authority before applying the replacement. Future-start commands retain the prior gripper setting until their start. Cartesian planning consumes the command's remaining time and cannot jump ahead after a delayed plan.

`actuation.py` maps desired effort through the existing actuator's affine force law, including its position/velocity bias and the original control/force clipping. It rejects unsupported transmissions or actuator dynamics; it never interprets a position-servo control value as torque and never changes the body, gain, damping, gear or actuator limits. Every effort sample reports the state-dependent reachable range and predicted clipped force. A synthetic native MuJoCo `mj_forward` fixture agrees with that algebra without stepping physics. This establishes an instantaneous mapping under the unchanged servo; it does not establish trajectory-level equivalence to a pure torque motor, ideal torque tracking, physical safety or complete-workcell feasibility. Those remain measured calibration duties. Cartesian IK/workspace checks are not an exact hidden-state collision oracle or a promise of collision-free motion.
