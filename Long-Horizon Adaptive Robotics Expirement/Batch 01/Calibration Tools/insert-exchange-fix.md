## Geometric diagnosis and proposed common calibration

The scene inserts are 50 × 50 × **16 mm**, with an approximately 8 mm centre height when settled. The fixture has **12 mm** high walls, whose inner faces lie 45 mm from fixture centre. The transport planner aims the TCP at the detected insert centre.

The pinned [Panda description](../../Runtime/vendor/panda/panda.xml) puts the TCP at hand-local z=103.4 mm, finger body origins at z=58.4 mm, and the main fingertip pad at finger-local `(0, 5.5, 44.5)` mm with half-size `(8.5, 4, 8.5)` mm. At full opening, each finger joint is 40 mm. In the declared vertical grasp orientation, the open pad occupies jaw-direction radii **41.5–49.5 mm**, crossing the fixture wall's 45 mm inner face. At TCP height 8 mm the pad extends down to approximately the table surface. The collision mesh `finger_0.obj` reaches local z=53.858 mm, or **8.858 mm below the TCP**, so that nominal target also puts part of the finger collision mesh about 0.858 mm below the table.

The A3-001 100 Hz motion stream during the first exchange leg records a lowest TCP near the fixture of `(0.566789, -0.000104, 0.018795)` m at 87.772 seconds, well above the requested insert-centre target. This is consistent with obstruction. It does not identify the complete contact sequence or prove one collision was the sole cause; the recorded contact peak is not a contact-pair classification.

The proposed geometry change keeps insert dimensions, mass/friction sampling, robot bodies, contacts and wall height. The two walls in the finger opening direction gain a **40 mm central jaw-access gap**: relative x is `(-0.020, +0.020)` m. Four remaining wall pieces have centres `(±0.0365, ±0.049, 0.006)` m relative to the fixture XY centre, with half-sizes `(0.0165, 0.004, 0.006)` m. Their x spans are `[-0.053, -0.020]` and `[0.020, 0.053]`; the two x-direction walls remain unchanged. The 50 mm insert is wider than the 40 mm access gap. Finger collision-mesh x half-width is at most 10.502 mm; adding the service's 4 mm reported position uncertainty leaves **5.498 mm** allowance inside a 20 mm half-slot. Tracking error, orientation error, contact deformation and dynamic settling still require measurement.

The insert-only TCP offset is **+4 mm at both source and destination**. Clamping the observed source centre to its public 8 mm half-height before adding the offset gives a nominal TCP minimum of 12 mm and approximately 3.142 mm mesh/table clearance. Applying the same offset at release preserves the intended insert body centre height; it does not make the planner move the body directly. Existing workpiece transport targets are unchanged. Unknown-payload recovery retains its identity-free 21 mm TCP release height: a held insert with this offset has nominal body-centre height 17 mm and bottom 9 mm above the plane, while the taller workpiece retains its existing clearance. Neither release is asserted successful before observation.

These are common arena/controller calibration changes for every entrant. They require new scene/source hashes and actual readiness measurements. No evaluator threshold is loosened, no insert is teleported or welded, and no object pose from the protected world enters the service planner.

## Service and machine safeguards in the proposal

The exchange request retains the old insert ID and intended home. After the first trajectory ends, the service waits at most two simulation seconds for a frame captured after that endpoint and no more than 0.35 seconds old. The old insert must be uniquely observed at its declared home, its public estimated XY bounds plus uncertainty must fit within the home region, its observed height must agree with tabletop placement within the stated uncertainty plus 3 mm, jaws must be measurably open, and no part or insert may be visibly occupying the fixture. Unseen old state remains unknown until the deadline. Contradictory fresh evidence fails the request. No replacement trajectory is dispatched on either failure. A request-correlated deadline cannot terminate a replacement command.

The successful public gate logs `setup_return_observed` with its observation time and basis, explicitly denying independent physical-success authority. The public transport planner also refuses to move an insert onto another visibly present insert. Multiple visibly installed inserts reject a setup request rather than arbitrarily selecting one.

A separate protected machine interlock requires the requested insert to be seated and **exactly one insert** to intersect the fixture's XY region and 0–80 mm vertical interlock volume. It counts overlap even when the other insert is moving or only partially within the region. This Boolean is checked at cycle admission, throughout processing and again before completion; loss faults the cycle and gives no process credit. The public rejection/fault exposes the failed interlock, not private coordinates or a hidden recommended action. The evaluator remains independent and unchanged.
