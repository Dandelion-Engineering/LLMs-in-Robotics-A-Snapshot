# Recovery options after the batch036 worker loss

Prepared 2026-09-24. This is a proposal, not a recovery authorization, retirement decision, changed result or readiness finding. No recovery implementation or further episode has been run by this work.

The [retained incident](incident.json), SHA-256 `6c67adb420ef7575c497f83918e96cdb01e02a025ceb45e911b621acb15cce4e`, records the authentic original queue disposition: job `20260924T201725179192Z-055d76ec` is **interrupted, exit status null**. Documented queue metadata repair removed its stale lease; it did not complete its physics or create its missing result. The suspected Windows process-liveness defect is supported by source and timing coincidence, not a direct trace identifying who terminated each process.

The present accounting is **132 finalized original attempts, one started/interrupted attempt without a terminal, and 131 unstarted original attempts**. Batch036 selected four: the interrupted `C24-rep2-A1-persistent` and three unlaunched C08 members. Partial evidence is retained. Task outcome, actual termination time and post-run integrity assessment are unknown/missing. Reconciliation time is not the physical termination time. The interrupted attempt belongs to the already excluded C24-rep2 block; that does not make its missing terminal disappear or prove that no other block was affected.

## Why ordinary r4 finalization cannot repair this

The frozen [Runtime 004 runner](../../../Runtime%20004/release_runner.py) has only `run`, `finalize` and `review` commands (lines 746–757). `finalize` requires the original job's successful/exit-zero receipt, matching installed state, `finished.json`, and exact protected terminal references (671–728). A new successful metadata job cannot substitute for the interrupted original. A continuation review additionally requires a retained terminal, recorded passing post-run integrity and finalized batch (518–528); unresolved started attempts block continuation (578). These are distinct requirements: finalization can preserve a failed integrity assessment, but review cannot waive it.

Do not construct legacy `terminal.json`, `finished.json` or `finalized.json` merely to satisfy those checks. Do not call `append_map_result` directly: it records official queue success/zero (699). Development `record_attempt.finish` rejects evaluation runs. A generic Map result without the protected terminal would be rejected by [the frozen collector](../../../Batch%2001/Release%20Analysis%20004/collect_results.py) (149–155). The already appended incident note is an honest existing recording path.

## Option A: retain r4 and add an explicit recovery version

**First, metadata recovery only.** A separately stored, versioned interrupted-attempt record and reader can preserve the 132 original finalized results and represent the additional attempt as administratively interrupted/infrastructure-invalid, with task outcome unknown and no invented duration, completion score or passing post-run check. Its evidence must bind the original release, selected batch plan, claim/registration/start, authentic interrupted receipt, partial-artifact inventory and process-absence observations. Preserve unknown physical termination time separately from known observation/reconciliation timestamps; the existing Map terminal schema requires an end timestamp and must not be satisfied with an unlabeled invented one.

The new reader would distinguish original finalized results, independently documented interrupted dispositions and genuinely unstarted manifests. It must leave the frozen collector and all historical files unchanged, preserve correction chains and exclusions, and identify this as a new provenance interpretation rather than ordinary r4 finalization. The legacy 004 reader can continue to report the attempt as missing. Any future canonical append needs this explicit interpretation and compatible reader first, not a workaround that breaks current evidence checks.

This metadata work is feasible without changing candidate policies, physics, protected manifests or original scores. It preserves useful analysis even if r4 never resumes. It does **not** by itself authorize or enable the remaining 131 episodes.

**Then, separately decide whether administrative continuation is supportable.** Completing the original manifest set without replaying the interrupted episode requires a prospective, versioned execution/recovery contract. The frozen runner cannot do this unchanged, and its authorizer verifies the actual queued entrypoint and frozen Runtime source set (198–224, 251–262, 368–374). An external wrapper, monkeypatch or forged legacy marker is not a solution. A candidate implementation would need explicit authority for the recovered disposition and new execution entrypoint, independent validation of all original identities, and preservation of the exact unstarted manifest set/order and zero-retry rule. Current post-incident verification can establish present bytes; it cannot invent the missing original post-run observation.

Before any such continuation, demonstrate that the approved queue repair works, identify whether its changed bytes affect frozen dependencies/readiness, and retain the actual checks required by that identity change. Do not assume a queue fix automatically leaves every frozen authority valid. Preserve model, prompts, controllers, physics, budgets, caps, qualification predicates, analysis and trace selection; do not inspect protected seeds or use comparative outcomes to choose changes. Treat the pre/post-repair execution periods explicitly in provenance and interpretation. A new administrative execution contract is a real amendment to the frozen provenance boundary, even if physical behavior is unchanged; continuing old manifests under it is not already supported by the existing runner or automatically exempt from the design's new-release rule.

The scientific benefit is retaining the 132 validly recorded attempts as evidence of their actual frozen system and avoiding an unnecessary replay of them. The cost is a transparent recovery-contract amendment and associated implementation/verification, plus possible infrastructure-period confounding. Never describe the combined set as an uninterrupted execution under unchanged original readiness. Preserve the earlier resource-sampler incidents and reviews independently; repairing queue liveness does not explain or repair GPU-observation timeouts.

## Option B: retire r4 and create a fresh release

The [evaluation design](../../../Long-Horizon%20Adaptive%20Robotics%20Experiment%20Design/Evaluation.md), lines 17–18 and 32, supplies this established route for changes after evaluation: retain the old evidence, make and verify a common fix, freeze a new release and create fresh concealed manifests. A fresh complete comparison has new identities; it does not retry or replace individual r4 attempts. Preserve the 132 results and interrupted attempt as historical evidence. Do not pool them into the new release's primary paired denominator or erase its 131 unstarted dispositions.

This has the clearest ordinary freeze/analysis semantics, but it repeats the complete 264-run comparison and may require fresh qualification if the chosen versioning changes physical-class identity. It does not follow merely from a missing terminal, and it does not make the existing evidence worthless.

It is presently **not feasible under the existing C-drive retention bound**. At the unchanged planning rate `3.189121856241325 MiB/task-second`, 156,600 task seconds plus the unchanged 50 GiB reserve require **577,363,228,951 bytes**. A small read-only `Get-PSDrive C` observation at `2026-09-24T20:42:36.2864541Z` returned **403,648,073,728 bytes free**, a **173,715,155,223-byte shortfall** while preserving the accumulated evidence. This supersedes only the dated free-space observation, not the policy or rate. Capacity must be resolved explicitly and measured again; do not delete or relocate evidence, shorten caps or drop runs to make the release fit. No storage remedy is selected here.

## Recommended next step

Keep r4 preserved and blocked while the separate shared-queue approval/verification proceeds. Prefer a bounded design of **Option A's metadata-only recovery and compatible reader**, because that directly accounts for the interrupted original and preserves the value of the 132 completed attempts without new physics, retrospective success or another 264-run storage commitment. Define its evidence and timestamp semantics before implementation and test rejection of forged success, altered identities, missing evidence and accidental episode retry.

Do not retire r4 merely because the old finalizer cannot express this failure. After that small design, make an explicit decision on whether a prospective administrative-continuation amendment can preserve the intended comparison and authority checks. If it cannot, retain the partial r4 analysis and consider Option B only with an actual common repair, adequate capacity and fresh evidence-bound readiness. Neither option permits silently weakening the frozen gates, manufacturing missing observations or automatically retrying an episode.
