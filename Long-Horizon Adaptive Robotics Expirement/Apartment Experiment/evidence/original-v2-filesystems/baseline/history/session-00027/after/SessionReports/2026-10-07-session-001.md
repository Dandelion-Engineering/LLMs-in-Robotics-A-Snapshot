## Learned constraints, procedures, or reusable knowledge

- A repeated upward release is effective for the observed orange sphere/bin geometry; the shorter durations brought the landing near center. Further geometry changes need new perception and experiments.
- Actual bilateral contact and lift feedback were essential for validating acquisition. Requested closure and a completed positioning request were insufficient.
- Completed base actions sometimes retained residual motion: yaw -0.3137 settled near -0.3598 after a turn; a completed 1.2 m drive's x=-1.070 settled near -1.105. Several further turns showed this effect. Wait briefly, then observe before precision use. This was selected for long-term memory.
- Lowering the held sphere to z=0.85 cleared `secure_lift_confirmed` while bilateral contact remained. The program therefore requires a prior high confirmed lift and checks contact at the low swing start.

## Failures and negative results

- Initial low-arm turn near the pedestal was blocked. Base motion invalidated earlier grasp coordinates; alignment was reacquired.
- The first positioning and closure did not acquire the sphere; a lift showed it remained on the pedestal. Correcting lateral alignment produced a confirmed grasp.
- The early horizontal candidate used endpoints x=0.60 and 0.94 at z=1.16, durations 0.12 and 0.15 seconds, holding width 0.055 then release 0.15. Far-short feedback was reported, but its timestamp preceded command completion and command-start freshness was not recorded. The loss mechanism is unresolved.
- A repeat preparation lingered at the low start pose before raising again and lost the object. The exact loss time and cause were not isolated. The reusable program uses only a brief low hold before the swing.
- `rg` was unavailable; filesystem discovery used `find`. One closeout patch was rejected for addressing the same file twice; it was corrected before application. No prior report was changed.
